# FILE47 — the work

**Sixty-five pieces**, filed under four disciplines. All of them are in the
drawers; **fourteen** of them also have a screen of their own in the room.

## The archive and the wall are two lists

They used to be one, and that was the bug: the drawers listed the pieces that
had been given a screen, so the thirty-odd other pages converted from the same
supplied files existed as files on disk and appeared nowhere a visitor could
reach them. The room has fourteen slots composed by hand against its geometry;
the archive has however many files the studio hands over. Tying the second to
the first meant the wall was silently deciding what the portfolio was.

So `WORK` is everything, and `PIECES` is the subset with a placement. A drawer
shows the whole shelf, wall pieces first. `file47-room.test.ts` asserts that
every drawer adds up to the whole archive and that nothing hangs that is not
filed.

## Filed by discipline

Four drawers: **LOGOS**, **PRODUCTION**, **CLOTHING**, **MARKETING**. They are
the folders the studio handed the work over in, not categories invented here —
`production` is the cannabis and packaging folder under the name the studio
uses for it, and the folder a file sits in is what files it. `scripts/file47-work.mjs`
reads one folder per discipline and refuses to guess from a filename.

A visitor arrives asking _do you do what I need_, and a shelf answers that in
one look; the client names sit one level down, where they are evidence rather
than noise. Nothing is filed in two drawers and no drawer is empty — both are
asserted, because either would be a promise the site cannot keep.

### Names came off the artwork, not the filenames

Every label above was written after opening the piece. The generator can only
uppercase a filename, and a filename is the studio's working name: `sick.png`
is a wordmark reading LOVSICK, `Untitled-1.jpg` is a Crystal Clear Water tee
set, `kc plumbing final.pdf` is a company whose artwork says **heating and
cooling**, and `chinese dat shit dragonEX .pdf` is DAT-STUFF's dragon pouch.
Shipping any of those as a label would be putting a name on a client that the
client never used. A piece with no entry in `LABELS` falls back to its filename
in caps — a test asserts none are shipping that way.

If any of these names is wrong, it is one line in `LABELS` in
`src/lib/file47-room.ts`. They are a reading of the artwork, not
information the studio gave us.

The two Crystal Clear Water sets are told apart by colourway sampled off the
artwork, not guessed: the tee body is **#06213e** on `new-jug` (navy) and a
neutral **#d2d2d2** on `untitled-1` (grey). If the studio's own colourway names
differ — ash, heather, bone — those are better labels and belong here.

**`cu-brain` is cropped.** The supplied PNG is an artboard: a stray black
fragment against the left edge, a 607px empty gutter, then the two lockups in
the right third, with the top half of the sheet blank. Hung whole it would be a
panel of mostly nothing, so the piece is the lockups — `x 883–1755`,
`y 390–761` of the original, plus a small white margin. The fragment is not in
it. If that fragment is part of the work rather than artboard litter, say so and
it goes back.

## Adding work

**From a browser.** Open [`work-source/`](../work-source) on GitHub, go into the
discipline the piece belongs to, press **Add file → Upload files**, drag the
files in and commit. A GitHub Action converts them and commits the results;
Vercel redeploys off that commit. Nothing to install and nothing to run — see
[`work-source/README.md`](../work-source/README.md).

**From a machine**, for files over GitHub's 25 MB web-upload limit, or offline:

```sh
pnpm work
```

Same converter either way. It reads `work-source/<discipline>/`, writes both
sizes for every piece into `public/work/`, and writes a manifest at
`src/lib/file47-work.generated.json` carrying the id, the section, the paths and
the true master dimensions. The site reads that manifest directly, so new work
reaches the drawers with no code change.

**The originals live in the repository.** `work-source/` holds every supplied
file, so the site can be rebuilt from its own sources: a clean checkout plus
`pnpm work` reproduces `public/work/` byte for byte. That was verified — 130 of
130 files identical — and it is the reason the sources are committed rather
than kept somewhere else.

The converter deliberately stops short of two things: where a piece hangs in
the room is composed by hand against the geometry in `file47-room.ts`, and the
label is a reading of the artwork — neither is a script's to guess. Run
`node scripts/unnamed.mjs` to list the pieces still waiting for a name; the
Action prints it in every run summary.

What it will not do:

- **Upscale.** The master is the source's own pixels capped at 2560, never
  stretched past them. `file47-zoom.ts` caps zoom at `master.w`, so an inflated
  number here would become invented detail about a client's work on screen.
  Sources under 768px get no master at all and the plate reads FULL SIZE.
- **Hang a blank.** Print files carry blank backs, dielines and trim slivers.
  Each rendered page is measured for ink and anything under 1.5%, or thinner
  than 6:1, is left out and named in the report rather than dropped silently.
- **Ship the same picture twice.** Sources are compared by digest, so a file
  handed over under two names, or filed in two folders, becomes one piece.
- **File by guesswork.** The folder a file sits in is what files it. A file
  loose at the top of `work-source/` converts but is reported as unfiled and
  does not appear in a drawer until someone says which shelf it belongs on. A
  folder that is not one of the four disciplines is named in the report and
  skipped, so a typo is visible rather than silent.
- **Guess at a format it cannot open.** `.ai`, `.psd`, `.eps`, `.indd` and video
  are reported by name with a note to supply a PNG export.
- **Take a whole deck.** At most six pages of any one PDF. A seven-page brand
  book is not seven portfolio pieces; if a later page matters, name it.

PDFs render through `pdftoppm` (poppler-utils) at a DPI computed from the page's
own size, so a business card and a banner both land near 2560px on the long
edge. The Action installs it; locally, `apt install poppler-utils`.

## How they were prepared

**Two sizes, two jobs.**

_On the wall_ (`/work/*.webp`) a piece lands on a CRT face a few hundred pixels
across and then goes through an ordered dither, so a 2560px master is pure
download for no visible gain. Each is **512 on the long edge**; all sixty-five
come to **1.4 MB**, and only the fourteen hung ones are preloaded with the room
— the rest are drawer thumbnails and load lazily.

_Opened_ (`/work/hd/*.webp`) is a different job. Selecting a piece opens it in
DOM at the device's own resolution with pinch, wheel and drag — see the plate
in `docs/BUILD.md` — and a visitor deciding whether to hire the studio wants
to read the fine print. These are the same images at up to **2560 on the long
edge**, quality 0.88, **9.5 MB in total**, and they are fetched **one at a
time, only when a piece is opened**. None of it is on the critical path.

Both sizes are flattened onto white first. Several sources are line art on
transparency, and a transparent texture on an unlit plane reads as a hole in
the monitor rather than as paper.

## Superseded findings

Kept because they were written down as facts and are no longer true.

- **"The DAT-STUFF pages have no HD master."** They do. That finding came from
  rasterising through the browser's PDF engine, which returned a solid fill
  above the reference size. Measured against the same file, poppler returns
  **21% ink**. Every piece in the archive now carries a real master.
- **The MO-DEM adult-content decision of 2026-09-12** concerned `mdm-collection`
  and its siblings, which are not in the archive any more — that set was
  replaced wholesale by the folders above. The decision stands if those pieces
  are ever supplied again.

## Regenerating

Re-running the pipeline rebuilds `/work` and the manifest from `SRC` and
nothing else; ids are derived from filenames and page numbers, so a piece keeps
its id and its place on the wall across runs. If a supplied file is renamed its
id changes, and a slot in `HUNG` naming the old id will **throw at module load**
rather than hang an empty panel — that is deliberate. Check the report for
pieces left out, and check the wall still carries ink: a blank panel is silent,
and the e2e asserts each tile's image actually decoded.
